1. Legibility and Quality
Ensure the floor plan is clear, with no blurred or missing sections.
Labels and text (e.g., room names, dimensions) should be readable.
Lines and boundaries should be well-defined and not broken.
2. Annotation and Labeling
All rooms and spaces should be labeled with their respective names and dimensions (e.g., "Kitchen 14.8 m²").
Measurements should be provided in standard units (e.g., meters or feet).
3. Scale and Proportions
The scale should be mentioned or visually consistent.
Proportions of rooms, walls, and furniture should make sense (e.g., no oversized doors compared to the room).
4. Completeness
Ensure all key spaces (e.g., living areas, bedrooms, kitchen, bathroom) are present.
Additional features such as balconies, hallways, or utility rooms should be appropriately included if applicable.
5. Architectural Accuracy
Walls, doors, and windows should be accurately represented with clear symbols or labels.
Staircases (if any) should be correctly shown with appropriate labeling.
6. Orientation
Check for indications of direction (e.g., north arrow) if the orientation is critical for processing.
The layout should follow a logical flow (e.g., bedrooms near bathrooms).
7. Furniture and Fixtures
Furniture placement should align with the room type (e.g., sofa in the living room, bed in the bedroom).
Fixtures like sinks, stoves, and toilets should be included where necessary.
8. Consistency
The room dimensions and areas mentioned should be consistent and realistic.
No overlapping or conflicting information (e.g., two areas labeled as the same space).
9. Language and Symbols
The language of labels should be supported by your LLM.
Symbols and conventions (e.g., for doors, stairs, or furniture) should align with architectural standards.
10. Metadata
Check for any additional metadata like the building name, project title, or creator information, if needed for classification.
